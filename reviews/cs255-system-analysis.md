# CS-255: System Analysis & Design

I worked on this DriverPass project back when I was trying to figure out how to turn a client's vision into a actual software blueprint. The goal was to help people study for driving tests and book lessons online.

## what it was
A system analysis and design project for DriverPass, a driver education platform. It focused on translating client requirements into a structured software plan, covering everything from user login to scheduling lessons and managing instructors.

## what holds up
My ability to break down a complex, multifaceted problem into smaller, manageable components was pretty solid. I used a reverse engineering approach—starting with the absolute necessities like authentication and then building out the rest of the modules—which kept the design from getting overwhelming.

## what I'd refactor
I definitely struggled with the visual side of things. I spent way too much time in the code and not enough time on the diagrams. If I were doing this now, I'd spend much more time in Lucidchart or even just sketching them out properly before touching a single line of requirements. A design is only as good as the blueprint it provides, and my blueprints were a bit messy.

## portfolio take
A good exercise in requirements gathering and system modeling, even if my diagramming skills were lacking.
